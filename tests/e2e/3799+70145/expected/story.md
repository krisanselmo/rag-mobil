# The Lantern at the Bottom of the Bay

That morning, the bay of Palm Tree Island was as blue and still as a mirror. In the middle of the water, Pépin, the pirate with the red vest and the long black ponytail, was watching the fish from his little brown boat. Perched on his shoulder, Coco, the blue parrot with yellow wings, was looking into the water too.

"Can you see them, Coco? Can you see them?" whispered Pépin.

To see the fish better, he leaned over the water… and splash! His lantern slipped off the bench and fell into the sea. It sank and sank, until it disappeared right at the bottom.

"My lantern!" cried Pépin. "I won't be able to explore the caves at night any more!"

On the beach, Tom, the red-haired castaway, and Robinson, the wise old man with the long white beard, came running.
"It's far too deep," sighed Robinson. "Even the turtles struggle to swim down that far."
And nobody on the island could dive that deep.

Suddenly, the water near the rocks began to bubble. Blop, blop, blop! A funny torpedo-shaped machine, with two little wings and a propeller spinning at full speed, shot up through the bubbles. It was an underwater scooter!

Holding on to its handlebars, a diver took off her dark grey masked helmet and shook her head with a smile.
"Hello! My name is Marine. I explore the bottom of the bay. I heard a big splash… Is something wrong?"

Pépin told her everything, waving his arms about.
"A lantern at the bottom of the sea? No problem," said Marine. "That's my job!"

She checked her diving vest and her grey air tank, joined to her mask by a black hose. Then she put on her flippers and her helmet.
"Are you sure it isn't too deep?" asked Tom, worried.
"With my scooter, I can go very deep," she answered. "See you soon!"

And she dived back down in a cloud of bubbles.

At the bottom of the bay, everything was blue and silent. Marine glided between the grey rocks, peering into every nook through her mask. No lantern…

All of a sudden, a little dark blue fish with a pale belly blocked her way. It had a long pointed nose: it was a swordfish!
"Hello, little swordfish," said Marine in a cloud of bubbles. "You haven't seen a lantern, have you?"

The swordfish swam around her twice, then darted off between the seaweed. Marine followed it, kicking her flippers. It led her to two sea turtles, brown and peaceful, resting on the sand.

The turtles raised their heads and swam slowly towards a big rock. Under the rock was the way into a cave. And there, stuck against the stone, something was shining silver and yellow: Pépin's lantern!

Marine pulled gently, once, twice… At last the lantern came free.

A few minutes later, the underwater scooter came back up near the beach. Marine held the lantern up high.
"Here it is!"
"Hooray!" cried Pépin, dancing on the sand. "Coco, our lantern is saved!"

To thank the diver, Robinson filled the basket with coconuts, and Tom brought out the wooden kitchen utensils: the spoon, the ladle, the fork and the bowl. Together they made a delicious coconut soup.

When the sun went down, Pépin lit his lantern on the beach. Its yellow light danced on the waves, and everyone ate, laughing and telling stories of their adventures.
"Will you stay the night on the island?" asked Tom.
"I'd love to!" answered Marine.

That night, the diver fell asleep in the hammock of the hut, rocked by the sound of the sea. And deep down at the bottom of the bay, the swordfish and the two sea turtles watched over the island.
